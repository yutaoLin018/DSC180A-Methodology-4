## Quarter 2 Project Reflection

### 1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?

The most interesting topic I learned about this quarter is how different environmental factors influence wildfire risk. Before working on this project, I mainly associated wildfires with high temperatures and drought. However, I learned that vegetation conditions, land cover, terrain, and weather can all contribute to wildfire risk. I found it particularly interesting that some factors, such as terrain, remain relatively stable over time, while others, such as temperature and vegetation conditions, can change significantly. Understanding how these factors interact is important for predicting wildfire probability and assessing how it might change under different climate conditions.

### 2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.

For the Quarter 2 Project, I would like to investigate how climate change and natural climate variability affect wildfire probability in the Iberian Peninsula. In particular, I am interested in examining how changes in temperature, precipitation, and vapor pressure deficit (VPD) influence predicted wildfire risk. By using our prototype model under different climate conditions, we could estimate baseline wildfire probability and examine how that probability responds to long-term climate changes and shorter-term natural variability. I think this investigation could help us better understand the relationship between climate conditions and wildfire risk.

### 3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?

One change I would consider making to our Quarter 1 approach is placing greater emphasis on how wildfire probability responds to changes in climate-related variables. In Quarter 1, our main objective is to build a prototype model using historical environmental and wildfire data. For Quarter 2, I would like to extend this approach by evaluating the model under different climate conditions rather than focusing only on its predictive accuracy. For example, we could examine how predicted wildfire probability changes when temperature, precipitation, or VPD varies, while accounting for other environmental factors such as terrain and land cover. This could make the model more useful for understanding the potential impacts of climate change.

### 4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?

I would be interested in using SHAP and climate scenario analysis to better understand how environmental variables influence wildfire probability. SHAP could help us interpret the contributions of different features, such as temperature, precipitation, VPD, vegetation, and terrain, to the model's predictions. Climate scenario analysis could then allow us to examine how predicted wildfire probability changes under different climate conditions. Combining these techniques could help us move beyond simply predicting wildfire occurrence toward understanding how climate change and natural variability may affect wildfire risk.
